# Open Research Questions

These questions are unresolved. Their inclusion defines a research agenda, not an implementation or capability claim.

## 1. Human change versus model degradation

How can we distinguish genuine human change from degradation of the system's model of that human?

Relevant complications include sparse evidence, context-dependent behavior, shifts in the agent itself, and feedback effects caused by the interaction.

## 2. Structural-error attribution

How should structural prediction error be attributed across multiple cognitive modules?

A failed prediction may arise from perception, retrieval, context selection, a human-model assumption, inference, simulation, action choice, or environmental change. Candidate attribution is not causal proof.

## 3. Person-specific prediction

Can an AI reliably simulate a specific person's future response rather than a generic response?

Evaluation must separate memorized stereotypes and population priors from evidence-supported individual adaptation, including under change over time.

## 4. Shared-workspace value

Does a shared workspace architecture measurably improve an LLM-based system, or merely make the architecture more theoretically elegant?

The answer requires controlled comparisons against simpler orchestration under matched quality, latency, and compute budgets.

## 5. Human autonomy and dependency

How should we measure human autonomy and dependency without reducing them to misleading single-number scores?

Potential dimensions include informed override, reversibility, diversity of options, correction success, skill retention, reliance patterns, and subjective experience.

## Additional questions

- What evidence threshold should permit a proposed model delta to become persistent?
- How should consent, expiry, correction, and deletion work for inferred representations?
- When does a clarification provide enough expected value to justify interruption?
- How should calibration be measured when the person and environment are non-stationary?
- Which experimental designs can detect sycophancy and self-reinforcing inference loops over long horizons?
